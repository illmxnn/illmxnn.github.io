About me

When I catch myself doing the same thing for the third time, I want to simplify the process. That’s how I started putting together my own automations.

From desktop to iPhone

I got into automation before I had an iPhone. On my computer I was building catalogs, searching archives, and preparing videos. My phone got me thinking about how to fit a few everyday steps into one tap.

How I check the result

I look at what happens on the next run. When moving a catalog, I checked the columns, prices, and image names, then compared the output files. With the same source data, the export should match byte for byte.

What I’m exploring now

On iPhone, I’m interested in shortening little chains of actions. A good target is a shortcut I can launch from the Home Screen and immediately see what it did.

Automations

Open any row to see what started the project and how it turned out.

01 Moving a catalog to a CRM A repeatable export from a website JustCars

Moving a catalog by hand meant standardizing prices, keeping track of photos, and shaping the table for the importer. I combined those steps into one export that checks the columns before writing the file.

One run prepared 170 entries and 4,844 photos. Running the same data again produced the same file.

02 Searching documents offline PDFs, contracts, and scans Archive

I built a local app that searches text across an archive. It reads text from scans, highlights the match in the original document, and lets me export the excerpt.

I tested recognition on a batch of 160 files. On the next run, the app indexes only new or changed documents.

03 Organizing a large store Drag-and-drop sorting and autosave LedStil

I organized more than 2,500 products across over 500 categories. Each category has its own product attributes, and products can be reordered by dragging them into place.

Changes save automatically. The Ukrainian and Russian storefronts share one catalog.

04 One video, several clips Cropping, captions, and dubbing ContenQ

A source video goes through transcription, cutting, cropping, captioning, and dubbing. The short versions can be exported in 9:16, 4:5, and 1:1 formats.

The finished clips can go straight into the publishing calendar with one click.

05 A publishing calendar Scheduling, approval, and posting PubLane

I built a calendar for scheduling posts in the right time zone, choosing brand accounts, and sending clips for approval ahead of time.

The schedule and approval status are visible in one place. Posts go out through official platform integrations.

06 Automations on iPhone Several steps in one tap Everyday

On my phone, I’m interested in shortcuts for small tasks. The Shortcuts app can chain a few actions and launch them from the Home Screen. This is where examples of my own shortcuts will go.
